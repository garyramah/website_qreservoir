Reservoir computing is emerging as an ultra-efficient architecture for physical quantum systems. Feed a signal into a fixed quantum system, let its non-linear dynamics do the work, and train only a simple linear readout. No backpropagation through quantum circuits. No training the internal recurrent layers. That is the whole trick — and it runs on today's hardware.

One platform, three surfaces

Whatever stage you're at — exploring the idea, benchmarking a device, or shipping a product — there's a surface for it.

01 / API

QRC APIs

POST a time series, get reservoir embeddings and predictions back. Managed reservoirs on simulated and hardware backends, with readout fitting included. No quantum expertise required to call it.

02 / SIMULATE

Simulation tooling

Build and benchmark reservoirs before you touch hardware: superconducting, photonic, Rydberg-atom, and neutral-atom models, with noise, finite sampling, and fading-memory diagnostics built in.

03 / ENGINE

Quantum tensor engines

Tensor-network backends that simulate large reservoirs classically — so you can prototype at scales no device can reach yet, then port the same reservoir definition to hardware.

Why reservoirs win right now

No barren plateaus

Variational quantum circuits die on flat loss landscapes. Reservoirs never optimize the quantum part at all — there is nothing to plateau.

Small-data strong

When samples are scarce — early trials, rare events, expensive measurements — QRC embeddings have beaten raw features and classical embeddings, with lower variance.

NISQ-native

Reservoir schemes are designed for noisy, intermediate-scale devices: tens of sites, shallow circuits, measurements as the interface. The hardware of today, not of 2035.

Where it applies

Forecasting chaotic dynamics

The benchmark task for reservoirs: predict systems that defeat ordinary models, from weather-like signals to driven quantum systems.

Molecular & materials prediction

Small-data property prediction — the QuEra / Merck / Amgen / Deloitte case study used QRC exactly here.

Quantum dynamics & state tasks

Recognizing entanglement, tracking quantum states, and classifying phases — inputs the reservoir can take natively.

Signal processing & sensing

Waveform classification, time-dependent signal processing, and sensor streams where memory of the past is the feature.
